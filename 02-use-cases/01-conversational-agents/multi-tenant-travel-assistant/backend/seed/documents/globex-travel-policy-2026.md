# Globex Corporation — Corporate Travel Policy 2026

Document version 2026.1 · Owner: Global Travel & Expense · Effective 1 January 2026

*Fictional document for this sample. Not a real corporate policy.*

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy applies to all Globex employees and contractors travelling on company
business, including travel arranged on their behalf by a designated travel
arranger. It covers air, hotel, ground transport, and ancillary expenses.

Where this document and the booking tool disagree, this document governs and the
discrepancy should be reported to the Travel & Expense team.

## 2. Air travel

### 2.1 Cabin entitlement

Employees book economy class by default.

**Business class is permitted on every fourth international trip within a
calendar year.** The entitlement is intended to offset cumulative travel burden
rather than to reward any single journey, which is why it accrues by trip count
rather than by flight duration or distance.

Trips are counted per calendar year and reset on 1 January. Unused entitlements
do not carry forward. A trip that has been booked but not yet taken counts
towards the total from the moment it is ticketed — this prevents two long trips
booked in the same week from each claiming the benefit.

### 2.2 Exceptions to cabin entitlement

The following circumstances may justify a cabin upgrade outside the normal
entitlement. All require written approval from a VP or above **before** booking:

- a documented medical requirement, supported by occupational health;
- a flight departing within 12 hours of a business-critical presentation where no
  economy seat remains available;
- travel accompanying an executive at Grade 12 or above where the itinerary
  requires joint working in transit.

Retrospective approval is not granted. An unapproved upgrade is treated as a
personal expense.

### 2.3 Ancillary air expenses

In-flight wifi is reimbursable on flights exceeding three hours. Seat selection
fees are reimbursable up to USD 60 per segment. Checked baggage is reimbursable
for trips of three nights or longer, or where equipment is being transported.

Lounge access is not reimbursable unless it forms part of the fare.

### 2.4 Fare selection

Book the lowest logical fare that meets the itinerary. A fare up to USD 150 above
the lowest available may be selected where it materially improves the schedule —
for example, avoiding an overnight connection or a second stop.

Refundable fares are permitted and require no additional approval.

## 3. Accommodation

### 3.1 Rate caps

The standard nightly cap is **USD 250** excluding taxes and fees, and properties
above four stars are outside policy.

City-specific exceptions apply where market rates make the standard cap
impractical. For New York, San Francisco, and London the cap is USD 340. These
exceptions are maintained by the Travel & Expense team and updated twice yearly.

### 3.2 Rate cap exceptions

Where a conference or event has caused local rates to exceed the cap across all
available properties, book the lowest available compliant option and note the
circumstance in the expense claim. Documented rate inflation of this kind does
not require pre-approval.

Where a client has requested accommodation at a specific property, the client's
preference takes precedence over the rate cap. Record the request in the booking
notes.

### 3.3 Reimbursable extras

Breakfast is reimbursable where it is not included in the room rate. Laundry is
reimbursable on trips of five nights or longer. In-room minibar charges are not
reimbursable. Room upgrades are not reimbursable.

## 4. Ground transport

Use ride-hailing or public transport for airport transfers. Rental cars are
appropriate where the itinerary involves multiple sites in one day or where no
practical public transport exists.

**Decline the rental collision damage waiver** — corporate insurance covers
company-booked rentals, and purchasing the waiver duplicates cover at personal
cost that will not be reimbursed.

Electric vehicles are preferred where available at comparable cost. Charging
costs are reimbursable.

## 5. Approval and escalation

Routine bookings inside policy require no approval. Bookings outside policy
require approval from the traveller's manager, recorded before travel.

Where a traveller believes policy prevents a legitimate business need, escalate
to the Travel & Expense team rather than booking outside policy and seeking
retrospective approval. The team can grant a documented exception in advance;
they cannot reliably do so afterwards.

## 6. Duty of care

Travellers must ensure their itinerary is recorded in the corporate travel tool,
including any segments booked directly. In a disruption or emergency the company
can only assist with travel it can see.

Personal travel extending a business trip is permitted at the traveller's own
cost, provided the business portion remains the lowest logical fare and the
itinerary is declared.
